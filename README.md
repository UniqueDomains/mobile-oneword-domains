## 🧱 Field summary

- `domain` — Fully qualified domain name.
- `status` — Current acquisition state for the domain in the public extract.
- `purchase_price` — Visible purchase price when available.
- `renewal_price` — Visible renewal price when available.
- `attractiveness` — Composite naming score used as a decision-support signal.
- `demand` — Relative buyer-pressure score when available.
- `length` — Character count without the TLD.
- `registrar` — Registrar name when known.
- `created_at` — Creation timestamp when known.
- `expires_at` — Expiry timestamp when known.

## ⚠️ Methodology and caveats

This selection is defined by a single trait: every domain uses the .mobile extension. That makes the comparison straightforward. The main question is whether the word becomes stronger or weaker when paired with a mobile-first suffix. Names like obey.mobile and zest.mobile feel brand-oriented and concise. Others, such as Liam.mobile or Cindy.mobile, may suit personal brands but can raise rights or attribution questions depending on intended use. Generic words like would.mobile or feels.mobile are easier to assess on memorability, clarity, and commercial fit. With a median ask of 373.25, price discipline matters, but extension relevance matters more.

- Check if the word gains meaning from the .mobile extension
- Favor short, clear words with strong recall
- Review names tied to people, brands, or characters carefully
- Use median ask 373.25 as a valuation anchor
